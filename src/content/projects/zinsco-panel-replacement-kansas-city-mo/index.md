The owners of a 1965 duplex in Kansas City, Missouri live in one side and rent out the other. A home inspection had flagged both units' breaker panels as Sylvania, a Zinsco design known as a fire hazard. They didn't want to lose any sleep over it, so they called us to replace both.

We replaced both 100-amp panels with new 100-amp, 24-space Siemens panels in a single day, and upgraded the grounding and bonding to meet Kansas City, Missouri's requirements.

## Why Sylvania and Zinsco panels are a fire risk

Zinsco panels were installed in many homes from the 1950s through the 1970s. GTE Sylvania later bought the company and kept selling the same design under the Sylvania name, so a Sylvania panel like these has the same problems.

- **Loose breaker connections.** Each breaker clips onto an aluminum bus bar. After decades of heating and cooling, those connections loosen and oxidize. A loose connection arcs and gets hot.
- **Breakers that melt onto the bus.** That heat can fuse a breaker to the bus bar. A fused breaker may not trip on an overload or short, and it may not turn off when you switch it off. That defeats the one job a breaker has.
- **Little warning.** Some panels show signs first: a warm cover, a burning smell, scorch marks or flickering lights. Many look fine right up until a breaker fails.
- **Hard to repair.** The panels have not been made in decades, and correct replacement breakers are hard to find.

Home inspectors routinely flag Zinsco and Sylvania panels, and some insurance companies will not cover a home that has one. The fix is to replace the panel rather than repair it.

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) panels with Stab-Lok breakers are another brand from the same era with a similar reputation for breakers that may not trip. If your panel says Zinsco, Sylvania or Federal Pacific, have it looked at.

## Planning a one-day swap for two units

Replacing a panel means the power company has to disconnect the service, and with a duplex both units go dark. We planned it so neither unit would be without power in the evening:

1. We pulled the permit with the City of Kansas City, Missouri.
2. Evergy's contractor de-energized the service in the morning.
3. With several electricians on site, we replaced both panels at the same time and moved every circuit to a new breaker.
4. The city inspected the work.
5. Evergy's contractor re-energized the service the same afternoon.

Power was off for about six hours. We told the owners early on to plan for up to seven, so both households could prepare.

## Grounding and bonding upgrades

Grounding and bonding tie the panel, the metal water piping and the earth together. If a fault energizes a pipe or a metal enclosure, the current has a safe path back to trip the breaker instead of staying on the metal. To meet KCMO requirements, we:

- **Bonded across the first break in the water line.** We installed a jumper across the first break in the metal water piping inside the house, where the water meter sits, so the bond holds even if the meter or a fitting is removed.
- **Added a second ground rod.** We drove an additional ground rod and connected it to the first with a jumper.

## Is your panel one of these?

Open the panel door and look for the brand name on the label or inside the cover. Zinsco and Sylvania panels often have breakers with colored handles (blue, red, green), like the ones in these photos. Federal Pacific panels usually say "Stab-Lok" on the label.
