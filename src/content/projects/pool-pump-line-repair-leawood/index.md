A plumbing crew digging up a Leawood, Kansas backyard to repair the main sewer line hit the conduit that carries power to the homeowners' pool pump. The excavator operator noticed and moved to a different spot, but the conduit was already broken. The plumber recommended us, and the homeowners called.

## What we found

The conduit ran about 14 inches below the surface and was broken where it crossed the excavation. The wiring inside had to be repaired before the hole could be filled back in, and the repair had to stay dry and stay accessible.

## What we did

- Set a rated PVC junction box where the line was broken
- Spliced the existing circuit inside the box with waterproof wire connectors listed for wet, exterior locations
- Pulled about 30 feet of new wet-rated wire to replace the damaged section, reusing the existing conduit instead of trenching a new run
- Finished the box at grade with gravel around it

## Why the box is at grade

The National Electrical Code requires a junction box to stay accessible: you must be able to reach the wiring without digging it up. A splice buried in the dirt with no way to get to it would not pass.

The homeowners also asked us to place the box where they can open it again in the future. If they add landscape lighting, the box gives them a ready connection point in the yard without trenching all the way back to the house.

## The result

The pool was already winterized, so we couldn't run the pump. Instead we confirmed the correct voltage at the pump, and the plumber could finish the sewer repair and backfill. Like all our work, the repair carries a one-year warranty, and we told the homeowners to call us if the pump doesn't start when they open the pool in the spring.
